# Copernicus

[中文](#中文) | [English](#english)

## 中文

本项目提倡数学评价的“哥白尼转向”：从追问“这项成果有多难、多新、多复杂”，转向追问“它为人的数学认识带来了什么”。

这一转向立足于数学作为人类活动的性质：无论研究、学习、教学还是应用，数学成果的价值，都应追问到它对人的认识所起的作用。因此，本项目以如下第一性原理为出发点：

> **数学成果服务于数学共同体对数学对象、结构与问题的认识。**

据此，评价需要辨析一项成果相对于已有认识带来了哪些增益，并以具体数学说明这些增益为何重要。这一主张称为[“哥白尼倡议”](Copernicus.zh.md)，供数学共同体讨论、采用与指正。

为将这一主张用于具体工作的评价，配套的[五维评价方案](Assessment-Scheme.zh.md)提出具体判据与图形表达，帮助说明贡献所在及其分量。方案可以随实践改进；评价的理由始终比等级与图形更重要。

倡议的持续推进离不开人和 AI 的共同参与。[MathMerit](https://github.com/GauSyu/MathMerit) 为这种协作提供工具：从有依据的 AI 阅读建议开始，人可以继续阅读、查证、提出异议，并共同修订评价。人的参与可以有不同深度，评价应如实说明其依据、实际核查范围与尚存疑点。

## English

This project advocates a “Copernican turn” in mathematical assessment: from asking “How difficult, novel, or complex is this work?” to asking “What does it contribute to human mathematical knowledge and understanding?”

This turn rests on mathematics being a human activity. In research, learning, teaching, and applications alike, the value of mathematical work should be traced to what it contributes to human knowledge and understanding. The project therefore takes the following first principle as its starting point:

> **Mathematical contributions serve the mathematical community's knowledge and understanding of mathematical objects, structures, and questions.**

On this basis, assessment should identify what a work adds to existing knowledge and understanding, and explain through concrete mathematics why those gains matter. This proposal is called [the Copernican Initiative](Copernicus.en.md), offered to the mathematical community for discussion, adoption, and correction.

To apply this proposal to particular works, the companion [Five-Dimensional Assessment Scheme](Assessment-Scheme.en.md) offers specific criteria and a graphical representation to help explain contributions and their weight. The scheme can evolve with practice; the reasons for an assessment remain more important than its grades or diagram.

Sustaining the initiative requires the joint participation of humans and AI. [MathMerit](https://github.com/GauSyu/MathMerit) provides a tool for this collaboration: starting from evidence-based AI reading advice, people can continue reading, checking, raising objections, and revising the assessment together. Participation can vary in depth; assessments should state their grounds, the actual scope of checking, and unresolved questions.
